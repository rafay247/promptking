# Creative, Naming, Copy and Design Results

## What an expert result looks like

A strong creative director gives you a few genuinely different directions, explains why each could work, recommends one, and keeps it grounded in what can actually ship.

- **Distinct directions.** Options that differ in strategy (descriptive vs evocative vs coined; benefit-led vs pain-led), not twenty variations of one idea.
- **Rationale.** One line per option on why it fits the audience and positioning.
- **A recommendation.** Name the pick and why.
- **Grounded.** Practical constraints are visible: pronounceability and spelling, likely availability of names, legal caution, real proof for claims.

## Quality bar

- [ ] 3–5 directions, each meaningfully different.
- [ ] Every option has a short rationale.
- [ ] A clear recommended pick.
- [ ] No invented facts: statistics, customer logos, testimonials and user counts appear as marked `[placeholders]` the person must supply or verify.
- [ ] Fits the product's audience and tone.

## Patterns by request type

- **Names (product, app, company)**: 8–12 strong candidates grouped by strategy, each with a one-line rationale; a top-3 shortlist with the recommended pick; a practical next step: check the domain, the app-store listing, social handles, and run a trademark search in the target markets before committing. Avoid names with awkward meanings for the category.
- **Landing page copy**: one recommended headline plus 2 alternatives; subheadline that states who it's for and the outcome; 3–4 benefit-led value props; how-it-works in 3 steps; proof section using marked placeholders; primary CTA; objection-handling line (price, time, privacy). Benefits over features.
- **Taglines, slogans, ad copy**: short options across tones, a recommended pick, and where each would work best.
- **Visual or UI direction**: a concept in words (mood, palette with hex values, type pairing, layout idea), why it fits the brand, and one alternative direction.

## Pitfalls

- Long undifferentiated lists without a recommendation.
- Weak-fit names for the category (connotations that suggest danger, delay or confusion).
- Fabricated statistics and fake social proof presented as real copy.
- Generic copy that could fit any product.

## Calibrating depth

- Quick brainstorm: grouped list with one-line rationales and a pick.
- Full page or campaign: complete copy blocks in page order, ready to paste.
